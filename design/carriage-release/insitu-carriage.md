# Insitu-style carriage and release

Prototype carriage / gripper concept follows Insitu’s ScanEagle SuperWedge idea (U.S. Patent 7,128,294, 2006), not a full copy of their hardware.

## What we take from Insitu

The patent separates the **acceleration run** from a **brake at the end of the stroke**. Gripper arms are linked to the carriage, so they hit that brake with it. When the carriage stops, the arms still have forward momentum and swing clear of the fuselage (patent Figs. 6A–6B).

For this launcher we use that same logic:

1. Arms hold the aircraft (football stand-in on the prototype) through the rail stroke.
2. A brake stops the carriage at the end of travel.
3. Arm inertia opens the grippers and clears the airframe; the brake also takes the stop loads into the carriage structure rather than yanking the fuselage.

## What we do not copy

The patent is not a pitch gate. It does not give spring rates, arm angles, or loads to reuse. Geometry, pads, and clearances for the Fighter / football fixture are ours to size and test (validation test B).

## Prototype note

Current bed is strut + trolleys with an Insitu-style gripper and a football as the fuselage stand-in. That validates hold / release timing and clearances, not the final carriage bearing or rail material.
